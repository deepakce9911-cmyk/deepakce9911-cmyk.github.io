# Dextr vs Canary AI Voice (2026): which AI should answer your hotel's phone?

**Short answer:** choose Dextr if the phone is where your bookings are won. Its AI takes the call, books the room and sends the deposit payment link before the call ends. Choose Canary if you are rolling out mobile check-in, guest texting and upsells across many hotels and want a phone agent added to that platform.

Dextr builds AI agents specifically for hospitality. Its AI voice agent, Daisy, answers a property's main phone line day and night. She checks what is available, quotes the rate, books the stay and sends a payment link for the deposit before the call ends.

Canary Technologies sells a web-based guest management platform: mobile check-in, mobile keys, upsells, guest texting, tipping and payments. In February 2025 it added an AI phone agent called Canary AI Voice.

Put simply, Canary added a phone agent to its check-in, messaging and upsell platform. Dextr built its AI around the phone call and the hotel work that follows it.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **Bookings:** both AIs take and change bookings in your booking software. Dextr's Daisy handles cancellations too.
> - **Deposits:** Dextr sends the deposit payment link during the call. Canary handles payment through separate products, such as payment links that hotel staff send by text or email.
> - **Handoffs:** both pass hard calls to a person with the caller's details. Dextr follows a clear rule: when unsure, hand over rather than guess.
> - **After the call:** Dextr runs housekeeping and maintenance with its own agent. Canary has no housekeeping or maintenance product; it sends tickets to other companies' tools.
> - **Outdoor stays:** Dextr has campground and RV park pages and case studies. Canary's site has neither.

> **Key takeaway:** both answer the phone and take bookings. The difference is what happens around the booking: Dextr asks for the deposit on the call, hands over carefully and runs the work behind the stay.

## Taking the booking: Dextr finishes the whole call

Picture a 90-room hotel at 9:40 pm on a Saturday. A guest calls about two rooms for next Friday, with a dog.

Daisy answers. She checks availability in your booking software, quotes the rate, answers the pet question from your own policy and books the rooms. Before the call ends she sends a payment link for the deposit, and the booking is confirmed. Nobody on your team picks up the phone.

Canary's AI Voice also answers around the clock, books the stay and can change an existing booking, and the booking appears in your booking software. It also promotes upsells during calls.

Dextr's agents offer extras too, across calls, texts and chat: late checkout, firewood or a golf cart. At two properties of a 50-plus property hotel chain, the bookings Dextr's AI took carried 15 to 17% higher average room rates.

> **Key takeaway:** both AIs can book a room. Daisy also handles cancellations and ends the call with the deposit link already sent.

## Getting paid: Dextr sends the deposit link before the call ends

A deposit is the money a guest pays upfront to hold a room. Guests who have paid one are less likely to cancel or not show up. The best moment to ask for it is while the guest is still on the phone and ready to commit.

Dextr does this inside the call. Its AI sends the caller a payment link before the call ends, so the guest can pay the deposit straight away. One family-run RV park took more than $9,474 by phone this way across 39 bookings. Dextr never stores raw card numbers; your payment processor, such as Stripe, secures them.

Canary collects payment with separate products. Hotel staff create a payment link and send it by text or email, and Canary can also generate links automatically from the booking's rate and status. Canary also offers an online card form and mobile check-in, where the guest adds a card before arrival.

> **Key takeaway:** with Dextr, asking for the deposit is part of the phone call. With Canary, the phone agent books the room and Canary's payment products handle the money.

## When the AI isn't sure: Dextr hands over rather than guessing

Every AI phone agent meets questions it should not answer alone, such as a wedding block, a refund or an upset guest. What matters is what it does next.

Dextr's rule is simple: hand over rather than guess. Daisy answers the questions she is sure of. Borderline ones she still answers, but flags for review. Anything outside what she knows goes to your team. At one RV park, Dextr's AI handed 415 of 1,419 calls and texts to the owners, with the guest's name, dates and request already captured, so they never started from zero.

Canary says AI Voice callers are "answered instantly or routed to the right person." One of its hotels says transferred calls arrive with the guest's details already captured, so staff know who is calling and why.

> **Key takeaway:** both pass hard calls to a person with the details attached. Dextr adds a clear rule: when unsure, hand over.

## Keeping it accurate: Dextr updates in plain English and checks every call

Your rates, policies and road closures change every week. Your AI has to keep up.

With Dextr, you describe the change in plain English, the way you would brief a new team member. At one RV park, the owners simply told Dextr that dogs over 50 lbs were now allowed, and the voice agent used the new rule on future calls. No support ticket, no engineer, no wait.

Dextr also checks the work. Every call is scored for tone, guest frustration and outcome, and calls that go wrong are flagged for review. Dextr pairs each client with a dedicated engineer and an AI strategist who keep improving the setup.

Canary starts with a knowledge base, a store of facts about your hotel, which grows as the AI talks to guests. Staff can rate each AI reply as good or bad, which Canary calls a thumbs up or thumbs down, and the AI learns from those ratings. Staff can also watch conversations live and switch the AI off.

> **Key takeaway:** with Canary, staff rate answers so the AI learns over time. With Dextr, you tell it the new rule in a sentence, and every call is scored.

## After the call: Dextr runs housekeeping and maintenance too

A booking creates work: a door code, a cleaning task, a pre-arrival message, a late checkout request.

Dextr has an agent for each part of that work:
- **Alfred** answers guest texts across SMS, WhatsApp and the booking sites. Guests can also check in and tip from their own phone.
- **Manny** builds the housekeeping board, assigns maintenance jobs and schedules staff from occupancy.
- **Cosmo** writes marketing emails and waits for your approval before sending.
- **A group sales agent** reads group and event emails and chases missing details.
- **DOSS** lets staff run operations by chatting with it in plain language.

Canary's guest-facing products include mobile check-in, mobile keys, a self-service kiosk, upsells, guest texting, room-service ordering and tipping. It also sells tools for group sales, payments and contracts. For housekeeping and maintenance, Canary's AI turns guest messages into tickets that staff approve and send to other companies' tools, such as Quore and HotSOS.

> **Key takeaway:** Canary has no housekeeping or maintenance product of its own. Dextr runs that work itself, with Manny.

## Campgrounds, RV parks and rentals: Dextr is built for them

Outdoor stays have their own questions: site types, hookups, pets, firewood, gate codes. Owners are often outside working when the phone rings.

Dextr has dedicated RV park and campground pages and two outdoor case studies. It connects to ResNexus and RMS Cloud, two of the major campground and RV park systems, and to dozens of vacation-rental systems, including Hostaway. Through RemoteLock, Dextr's agents can resend a guest's access code by call or text.

Canary has a vacation-rental page and a lakeside resort story near Yosemite. Its site has no campground or RV park pages.

> **Key takeaway:** for hotels, both are options. For campgrounds, RV parks and rentals, Dextr is the one built for the job.

## What a missed call costs you

Phone bookings still matter. Canary's own blog puts voice at about 18% of hotel reservations, and Revinate's data shows hotels convert about half (49.9%) of calls from people looking to book.

Many of those calls are never answered. Canary's AI Voice page says up to 40% of hotel calls go unanswered. In an AHLA survey published in March 2026, more than half of hoteliers said their properties were somewhat or severely understaffed.

Here is the maths for a hotel taking 60 booking calls a day, using a cautious 25% missed:

- 15 missed calls a day
- About half would have booked: 7 bookings
- At 2 nights and $150 a night: about $2,100 a day
- Over a 30-day month: about $63,000 in bookings at risk

Swap in your own call volume and rates. The point holds at any size.

A survey commissioned by Wyndham asked 325 hotel owners and developers across many brands in the US, Canada and the Caribbean about AI. The barriers they named most often were data privacy and security (46%), cost (42%) and connecting AI to older systems (40%). 57% said they need human oversight before AI makes operating decisions.

Dextr answers each one. It never stores raw card numbers and never uses your hotel's data to train AI models. Its pricing is based on use. It lists more than 50 live connections to hotel and rental booking software. And its AI hands over to a person whenever it is unsure.

> **Key takeaway:** both vendors agree that missed calls cost hotels money. Dextr is built to turn those calls into paid bookings.
